My research interests are in **complex systems and wicked problems** that require a fundamentally interdisciplinary approach.
From the information ecosystems in online social media to the accumulation of long-term health conditions in patients,
these problems require a combination of advanced modelling and numerical techniques that are grounded in wide knowledge
of the problem domain.

My contribution to the interdisciplinary mix is technical — I employ tools from **complex systems, network science, agent-based modelling, 
and Artificial Intelligence**. However, I give considerable care to approaching and understanding the other research fields
that are involved. Because **models need to be grounded in the phenomena they describe**, and only with substantial communication
across disciplines this is possible.

Therefore, I see my role as a **bridge between communities**: understanding, translating, and communicating needs, ideas, and 
methodologies in both directions. I can bring advanced methodologies to researchers in other fields, and bring their 
needs and particularities back to inspire new methods.

### Common pitfalls in attempting interdisciplinary research

#### One-sided modelling

Much of the work on opinion dynamics approaches the problem from a physics perspective, often **favouring mathematical 
tractability over realism** in how individuals are assumed to behave. Many models live in an "abstract realm", while the 
validity of their assumptions and findings depends on context. I believe models should be tied to specific phenomena, 
situated in time, space, and a social group, and validated either through solid foundations in other disciplines 
(cognitive science, psychology, social and political sciences) or through empirical data. None of this is possible 
without working closely with researchers from those fields.


#### Blind application of statistical methods

In my collaborations I have repeatedly noticed a **gap between how network and statistical methods are understood in 
theoretical fields and how they are applied** in areas such as medicine or political science. Closing that gap requires 
more than sophisticated methods. It requires translating and communicating them to other communities, making the tools 
accessible, and listening to what those communities actually need. Something mathematically 'correct' and sophisticated
may actually not be usable.


#### Siloed teams
I have also seen the limits of a **compartmentalised approach to multidisciplinary projects**, where teams from different 
disciplines work side by side but rarely talk to each other, and meaningful contributions get lost. Interdisciplinarity 
takes frequent exchanges, proactivity, and genuine effort to read and understand the literature and approaches of other 
domains.

### My interdisciplinarity research experience in practice

- **Political and social sciences.** Together with political scientists, I developed [a learning-based method](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/17457289.2020.1760282) for 
matching users to parties in voting advice applications. In my PhD, I used agent-based models to study how political 
party competition shapes consensus and polarisation in the electorate. I have attended the conference
  European Consortium for Political Research twice, where I have presented my work and engaged with the political science
community.
- **Medicine and healthcare.** At the University of Edinburgh, I was part of the AIM-CISC project, a large team of more 
than twenty researchers from six disciplines (computer science, medicine, sociology, biology, and geography), together 
with healthcare professionals and members of the public. I worked closely with clinicians to apply network science 
methods to the understanding the associations between long-term conditions, adapting the methodology to their needs. The
work has been published in medical journals and I have presented it to the medical research community.
- **Cognitive science and psychology.** I have worked on [opinion dynamics models built on cognitive models of structured 
beliefs](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-93631-9_3).
- **Computational Social Sciences.** At the AI, Media & Democracy Lab, I work along colleagues from communication 
sciences, psychology, and law to understand how algorithmic recommendation in social platforms affects disinformation 
spread and polarising dynamics. We have obtained an *interdisciplinarity grant* of 10,000€ for our project on how the 
private message behaviour of young users of TikTok may reduce polarising dynamics.

### Building interdisciplinary communities

Interdisciplinary research does not happen on its own. It needs spaces where people from different fields can meet, so I have often taken the initiative to create them:

- I organised the [AI for Multimorbidity Early-Career Researchers Interdisciplinarity Day](https://github.com/aim-rsf/AIM-ECR-network/tree/main/ECR-days/Edinburgh-24-Oct), including training on 
effective interdisciplinary collaboration and peer sharing of good practice, funded by a £10,000 grant from the [AIM RSF](https://www.turing.ac.uk/research/research-projects/ai-for-multiple-long-term-conditions-research-support-facility).
- I organised the [Symposium on Opinion Shift, Social Influence, and External Manipulation](/symposium-opinion-shift/) at the University of Edinburgh,
with the aim of bringing together researchers who study the topic from different perspective and disciplines. I invited 
speakers from computer science, psychology, and social sciences, and reached a mixed audience from those disciplines.
- I co-chaired the [Bridging AI and Psychology (BAIPsy)](https://xuerli.github.io/BAIPSY/) student workshop, creating a space of exchange for PhD and 
MSc students working or interested in AI, psychology, and the intersection of the two.
- I am currently a [IAS Next](https://ias.uva.nl/community/ias-next/ias-next.html) lead at the **Institute for Advanced Study** at the University of Amsterdam, where we 
contribute to maintain a research community for early-career researchers working on interdisciplinary topics.
- I co-founded and co-organised a cross-departmental **Networks Reading Group** at the University of Edinburgh.

#### Looking ahead

I advocate for a more interdisciplinary scientific approach, and I recognise that the **institutional context** is 
essential for it to succeed: interdisciplinary work needs time, funding, and recognition that traditional academic 
incentives do not always provide. My aim is to keep integrating my methodological expertise with the domain knowledge 
of researchers from other disciplines, to validate models against real phenomena, and to increase the significance and 
impact of research beyond the boundaries of any single field.

I very much welcome interdisciplinary connections, discussions, and new projects to work on. **My aim is to advance and 
lead research under these principles; and to contribute to spaces that allow for interdisciplinary integration.**
