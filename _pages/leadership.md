For me, leadership is about **creating the conditions for others to be their best**.
This means hearing, guiding, supporting, and coordinating efforts towards a common mission or goal.



---

### Research leadership

#### Research supervision
I enjoy delving into research ideas, bringing people into them, and working on them together. So far, this has mostly 
taken the form of research supervision (MSc students, [PhD project](https://www.mac-migs.ac.uk/), 
[Complexity72h project](https://www.complexitynextgen.org/complexity72h/previous-editions/london-2026/)), where I       
pitched project ideas that early-career researchers chose to join, and then guided them through. This has worked well, 
as shown by the **resulting papers** ([WIVACE](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-93631-9_3), 
[Complexity72h](https://www.complexitynextgen.org/complexity72h/previous-editions/london-2026/)) 
and **awards** (_Best Dissertation for the Data Science MSc_).

I regard supervision as a **co-creation** process, where I set up a problem and possible directions and then
discuss a path to take based on skills, interests, ideas, and resource constraints. I place importance on having regular
meetings to discuss methods and results and to hear about challenges and difficulties, so I can offer the right **support**.
I understand that people have different needs, working styles, and personal life circumstances, and I adapt to account
for these as much as possible.



#### Creating spaces for research interactions

The best ideas come from connecting what's already there in unexpected ways. Therefore, **breakthrough research needs
spaces for exchanging ideas** and points of view, particularly when they are quite **diverse** — e.g., with
[interdisciplinarity](/interdisciplinarity/), but also from different personal and life experiences.

**I enjoy creating points of connection** for researchers to disseminate and discuss research together. Already
during my PhD and postdoc, I created and guided meetings among PhD students within my research group to share their work
or other interesting papers. These efforts have later grew in scale: I co-founded and ran 
a cross-departmental reading group on network science (University of Edinburgh), co-chaired an event for psychology and AI
PhD students to share their research ([BAIPsy](https://xuerli.github.io/BAIPSY/)), co-organised my institute's internal 
research seminars ([AIAI seminars](https://web.inf.ed.ac.uk/aiai/events) 2022–2023)
and organised a [symposium](/symposium-opinion-shift/) about opinion dynamics bringing leading researchers from different departments to present 
their research and foster discussions.

### Community leadership

I consider that **researchers' life and wellbeing are more important than the research they do** — a weirdly bold statement.
Wellbeing involves having motivation at work, not suffering from overwork and burnout, and having a rich social environment
that can support them and provide a sense of belonging.

Therefore, I strongly value working at **institutions and environments that make people's wellbeing a
top priority**, while devoting resources to creating a healthy working environment where all sides of being human are 
welcome. Fortunately, this also leads to better research!

Alongside my research, I have consistently taken the **initiative to improve the working environment around me and create
peer communities**, from re-launching and leading the [Informatics Research Staff Society (IRSS)](https://www.wiki.ed.ac.uk/pages/viewpage.action?pageId=173007090) 
to serving on committees for other early-career communities ([IAS Next Lead](https://ias.uva.nl/community/ias-next/ias-next.html), Member of the [CWI Activity Committee](https://www.cwi.nl/en/intranet/social/activity-committee/)).
I have continuously striven to actively create spaces (from tea gatherings to career and social events) where people can 
connect, ask for help, share advice, or just have fun together.
I have also played a role in representing and advocating for the needs of such communities towards other stakeholders 
([IRSS](https://www.wiki.ed.ac.uk/pages/viewpage.action?pageId=173007090), Buildings committee, 
[AIM ECR Representative](https://github.com/aim-rsf/AIM-ECR-network), AIC PhD Academic Representative), 
making sure that everyone, especially those in more precarious positions, has a voice.
